## 1. What is HackHarvard?

HackHarvard is a three-day undergraduate hackathon. The 2026
presentation describes **36 hours of hacking**, **500+ students**, **30+
universities**, and **10 countries** (slide 3). Teams can have **up to
four members**, and solo participation is allowed (slide 6).

## 4. Sponsor challenges --- prizes and actual requirements

### Daytona --- Best Use of Daytona (slide 10)

-   Use Daytona's secure code-execution sandboxes in a meaningful part
    of how the project works.
-   Judged for creative and effective use.
-   **Prize:** \$5,000 in Daytona credits for the winner.
-   Workshop: Saturday, 2:30--3:30 PM.
-   **FEEL fit:** Weak unless sandbox execution genuinely serves a real
    product feature. Do not add it solely to qualify.

### SpaceX / Cursor --- Make It Legendary (slide 11)

-   Build a project using **real space data**.
-   Build with **Cursor** and use **Grok Imagine or Voice API**.
-   Bonus points for Grok Bot in planning/team collaboration.
-   **Winning team:** Branded Cursor mechanical keyboard per member.
-   **Every entry:** Raffle for a Cursor Owala bottle.
-   Workshop: Saturday, 1:30--2:30 PM.
-   **FEEL fit:** Poor for the current accessibility concept. Do not
    force a space theme.

### Chalk --- Best Data-Driven Agent Built on Chalk (slide 12)

-   Build an **agentic application** running on contextual or real-world
    data.
-   Use at least one Chalk component: sandboxes, AI gateways, data
    integration, or evaluations.
-   Judged on **impact, use of data, and evaluation quality**.
-   **Prize:** \$1,500+ in Apple gift cards for the winning team.
-   **FEEL fit:** Possible only if the actual application uses Chalk
    meaningfully and includes a genuine agent and evaluations. Merely
    using an LLM to name bookmarks may not qualify.

### Factory --- Best Use of the Factory Droid SDK (slide 13)

-   Integrate the **Droid SDK** into the running product using
    TypeScript or Python.
-   **Droid must do real work inside the product**, not merely assist
    with writing code.
-   README must explain where and how Droid is used.
-   **Prize:** \$2,000 cash plus \$200 in Factory credits for each
    member, and swag.
-   **FEEL fit:** Weak without a genuine in-product agent workflow.

### Capital One --- Best Use of Nessie (slide 14)

-   Use Capital One's Nessie REST API for a banking-related experience
    (accounts, transactions, ATMs, branches, etc.).
-   **Prize:** \$300 Giftogram gift card per member.
-   **FEEL fit:** Not relevant to current project.

### Photon 2.0 --- Build a Messaging Agent (slide 15)

-   Build an agent using Photon 2.0 and **go live on at least one Photon
    channel**.
-   Available channels described include iMessage, SMS, RCS, WhatsApp,
    Telegram, email, and calls.
-   **Winner:** \$400 cash, \$300 Photon credits, and fast-track to
    final interview round.
-   **Runner-up:** \$200 cash + \$100 Photon credits.
-   **Best Voice Agent:** \$300 cash + \$100 Photon credits.
-   Workshop: Friday, 4--5 PM.
-   **FEEL fit:** Only if messaging is a core user interaction, not an
    artificial integration.

### ASUS --- Content Challenge (slides 16, 41--42)

-   Share **public** content about building for the ASUS *Save the
    Earth* sustainability track.
-   Accepted platforms: Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube.
-   Tags: Instagram `@asususa` and `@hhuh.hackathon`; LinkedIn
    `@ASUS Education`; TikTok `@asuseducation`; YouTube `@ASUSUSA`.
-   Hashtags: `#ASUSEducation #hackharvard2026 #agentcomputer`.
-   Submit the **proof-of-content form** linked on hhuh.io.
-   **Prize:** Slides differ: slide 16 says "announced soon"; slide 41
    mentions a chance to win an ASUS ZenScreen portable monitor,
    **subject to change**. Confirm with organizers.
-   **FEEL fit:** Not applicable unless participating in the
    sustainability track.

## 6. FEEL --- project fit

**FEEL --- A Second Sense, Powered by AI.**

FEEL uses an iPhone 14 Pro Max, LiDAR/ARKit, Bluetooth, an ESP32, seven
FS0307 servos, and a 7 × 4 tactile-pin array to communicate spatial
information through touch.

### Development priorities

**P0 --- FEEL V2: Finish HANDOFF v7** - Complete the existing live
perception system: obstacles, doors/EXIT signs, target finding. -
Preserve the nine-byte BLE packet and 500 ms failsafe. - Complete iOS,
firmware, CAD, and simulated integration testing. - Validate real
iPhone, ESP32, servos, and physical pin mechanism when hardware
arrives. - Never describe a simulation as a physically verified result.

**P1 --- FEEL V3: Spatial Memory** - Save named ARKit spatial
bookmarks. - Recall direction after walking and turning. - Encode
direction through tactile pins. - Implement tracking-loss/uncertainty
behavior. - Develop in an isolated branch once V2 software-only gates
pass; do not destabilize V2.

**P2 --- Optional** - Persistent cross-session spatial memory, advanced
voice interaction, richer object finding, and new wearable hardware.

### HackHarvard demo

1.  Show a real obstacle or door producing tactile feedback.
2.  Show a target highlighted through the same tactile interface.
3.  If V3 is genuinely integrated, save a reference, turn away, and
    recall its direction.
4.  Show measured test results and explain limitations.

### Why Hardware for Good?

The challenge explicitly welcomes sensors, microcontrollers, and
wearables (slide 9). FEEL's hardware is integral to the user experience.
**This is the primary track to pursue.**

Do not force sponsor SDKs into the product just to chase prizes.

## 7. Submission and judging checklist

-   [ ] Ask organizers about pre-existing work and record the response.
-   [ ] Confirm team has no more than four members.
-   [ ] Confirm Hardware for Good entry selection and any track-specific
    form.
-   [ ] Ensure the project runs or has an honestly labeled simulation
    fallback.
-   [ ] Prepare a 60--90 second demo and a backup video.
-   [ ] Write a clear README: problem, approach, architecture, new work
    this weekend, limitations, how to run.
-   [ ] Record actual build/test commands and outputs.
-   [ ] Document physical safety limits; do not claim certified mobility
    assistance.
-   [ ] Prepare screenshots/photos and a short explanation of the
    tactile interface.
-   [ ] Submit to Devpost **before Sunday 8 AM ET**.
-   [ ] Be present for judging in Canopy Hall, Sunday 9 AM--noon.
-   [ ] Attend closing ceremony if participating, Sunday 3--4 PM.

## 8. Instructions for the coding agent

Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/HANDOFF-v7.md`, and this file.

1.  Treat the opening ceremony slides as the source for event dates,
    track descriptions, prizes, and venue rules.
2.  Prioritize **Hardware for Good**.
3.  Keep a clearly labeled list of pre-existing code/CAD and new
    hackathon work; do not make eligibility assumptions.
4.  Complete V2 software validation before isolated V3 work.
5.  Do not add sponsor integrations unless they genuinely serve FEEL and
    meet the challenge's exact requirements.
6.  Produce an honest Devpost-ready README, demo script, test report,
    and unresolved-questions checklist.
7.  Never invent test results, prize amounts, or organizer approvals.
